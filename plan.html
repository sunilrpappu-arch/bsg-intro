How each evening runs

The shape is the same every time, so nobody has to wonder what is coming. Three daimoku to open, three minutes of recall from last time, then the evening's topic, then a minute to name the homework and close. One topic per evening — nothing is shared between two nights, and nothing is split across them.

Saturdays run for an hour, and each one uses the extra half hour differently. The 12th gives Three Thousand Realms — the hardest topic in the bank — the room it needs. The 19th carries the lightest topic and then an open Q&A at the halfway point. The 26th is the timed paper, read together. Tuesdays and Thursdays are thirty minutes.

Rotate who leads. Give each of the four divisional members one or two evenings to present. Teaching a topic is the fastest way to own it — and it turns the group from a class into a study team.

If someone misses an evening, nothing is lost. Every evening is exactly one topic in the app, so they can take it in their own time and rejoin without falling behind.

A word on the Gosho passages

They are the second largest topic on the paper — 47 questions, 15% — and they are pure recall. Forty-seven quotations met for the first time on the evening of the 24th, three days before the exam, is the worst possible shape for memorising anything.

So although the passages have their own evening, the homework starts them from night three onward, one block at a time:

Block A — On Establishing the Correct Teaching for the Peace of the Land, and the spirit of Soka. Start after night three.
Block B — On Repaying Debts of Gratitude: the three virtues of parent, teacher and sovereign. Start after night four.
Block C — The Opening of the Eyes: pillar, eyes, great ship — and Toda's stand-alone spirit. Start after night five.

By night seven all three have been seen, and the evening becomes consolidation rather than first contact. Five minutes a day beats an hour on the last Saturday, every time.
